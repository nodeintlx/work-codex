# Investor Memo — NRG Bloom Facts and Numbers Conversion Layer

## Company Snapshot

- Company: NRG Bloom
- Headquarters: Montreal, Canada
- Operating geography: Nigeria, with category benchmarks drawn from global energy and compute markets
- Operating model: Modular, containerized compute infrastructure deployed against stranded or hard-to-deliver energy
- Current stage: Operating proof established; investor-grade market narrative now anchored to audited public claims

## Positioning

NRG Bloom should sound like the operator that can translate stranded energy, unreliable grids, and underbuilt digital infrastructure into a financeable compute platform.

## Core Investment Thesis

NRG Bloom becomes capital-worthy when investors see four truths together: real operating proof in Nigeria, a real power-delivery bottleneck, a permit-backed flare-gas monetization market, and a still-underbuilt data-infrastructure layer.

## Verified Proof Today

- NRG Bloom has been operational at Ogboinbiri in Bayelsa State since February 2025.
- NRG Bloom's Ogboinbiri site is described internally and on the investor page as 1.5 MW operational capacity.
- NRG Bloom's operating model is modular and containerized for remote energy-edge deployment.

## Why This Window Exists

The timing is stronger because IEA has made the energy side of the compute story explicit, World Bank materials still show Nigeria's structural electricity gap, and NGFCP plus public Nigerian flare-to-compute announcements have made the category more legible.

- IEA analysis in 2025 said electricity demand from data centres worldwide is set to more than double by 2030 to around 945 TWh.
- Recent World Bank materials describe Nigeria as having the world's largest electricity access deficit, with roughly 85-90 million people lacking access and annual economic losses from poor power supply in roughly the $26-29 billion range.
- Recent institutional analyses describe Nigeria's grid as delivering only roughly 3-5 GW on average despite materially higher installed or nominal capacity.
- 2025 market-intelligence coverage described Nigeria's third-party data-center market as still relatively small, with figures around 65.8 MW live and about 327.8 MW under construction or planned.

## Category Lineage

NRG Bloom is not asking investors to believe stranded-gas compute is a speculative category. The category logic is already visible in North America; the question is which operator can adapt it to African field conditions.

- By December 2025, NGFCP reporting said 28 firms had final flare-gas permits across 49 awarded sites, with 42 bidders involved, 250-300 mmscfd of gas to be captured, and nearly 3 GW of power potential.
- Green Flare announced a 53 MW flare-gas compute project in Nigeria in 2025, structured across three Delta State sites.
- In March 2025, Crusoe said it sold its bitcoin-mining operation to NYDIG after deploying more than 425 modular data centers across seven U.S. states and Argentina.

## Why NRG Bloom Could Win

NRG Bloom can win if it keeps sounding like a field operator rather than a futurist: show site proof, speak clearly about grid reality, and explain why modular deployment belongs in the same conversation as larger digital-infrastructure build-out.

## What Capital Unlocks

- Scale site deployment capacity from proof-of-concept operations into repeatable commercial infrastructure.
- Strengthen field operations, monitoring, partner onboarding, and logistics execution.
- Deepen commercial development with flare-gas counterparties and compute customers.
- Convert a live operating proof into an investable platform with disciplined expansion sequencing.

## What This Means For Investors

Best fit is investors who understand that energy constraints, remote operations, and modular execution are not side details. They are the business.

## Next Diligence Step

Move qualified investors from public content into the one-page brief, then into a diligence call centered on verified operating proof and market structure.
