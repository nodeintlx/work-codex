**Title:** NGFCP Turned Flare Monetization Into a Governed Market
**Channel:** Founder POV
**Pillar:** flare-gas

Flare-gas compute in Nigeria should not be framed like a fringe experiment anymore.

By December 2025, NGFCP reporting pointed to 28 final permits, 49 awarded sites, and nearly 3 GW of power potential.

Most people are reading this the wrong way.

This is what category formation looks like: regulation, public projects, and analogs that make the market easier to underwrite.

The more useful read is this:

NGFCP gives flare monetization a more legible commercial frame than most outsiders realize.

Green Flare's 53 MW announcement shows the Nigerian market is already attracting visible build-outs.

Crusoe's exit matters because it shows the model was real enough to become part of a larger power-and-compute story.

My read is simple: The interesting question is not whether flare-to-compute is real. It is which African operators investors will trust once the market becomes legible.

That is where NRG Bloom intends to win.

NRG Bloom intends to be read as one of those operators, not as a spectator.

What gives investors more conviction in stranded-gas compute: regulation, basin economics, or proven field execution?
