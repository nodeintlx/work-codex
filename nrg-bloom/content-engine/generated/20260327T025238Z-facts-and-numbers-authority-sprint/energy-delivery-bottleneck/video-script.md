# Short Video Script
**Title:** The Constraint Is Not Demand. It Is Energy Delivery.
**Format:** 45-60 second talking-head or kinetic text

## Hook
If your compute thesis ignores energy delivery, it is missing the bottleneck.

## Body
IEA says data-centre electricity demand could more than double by 2030. World Bank materials still describe Nigeria as having the world's largest electricity-access deficit.

Most people hear that and think market size.
The operating takeaway is straightforward:

The hard part is not whether demand exists. The hard part is whether energy can actually be delivered where compute can use it.

First: Compute is moving into a period where electricity availability matters more than slideware.

Second: Nigeria forces you to separate energy supply from energy delivery. They are not the same thing.

Third: NRG Bloom's model matters because it goes to trapped or wasted energy instead of waiting for the grid to behave.

The market does not need another polished Africa data-centre narrative. It needs people who understand that if power cannot be delivered, compute does not happen.

## Close
The winners in this market will be the teams that know where energy can actually be turned into compute.
