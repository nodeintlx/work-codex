# X Thread

1/ Most people still talk about African compute as a demand story. That misses the real problem.

2/ IEA says data-centre electricity demand could more than double by 2030. World Bank materials still describe Nigeria as having the world's largest electricity-access deficit.

3/ This is where the market still gets lazy. The hard part is not whether demand exists. The hard part is whether energy can actually be delivered where compute can use it.

4/ Compute is moving into a period where electricity availability matters more than slideware.

5/ Nigeria forces you to separate energy supply from energy delivery. They are not the same thing.

6/ NRG Bloom's model matters because it goes to trapped or wasted energy instead of waiting for the grid to behave.

7/ The market does not need another polished Africa data-centre narrative. It needs people who understand that if power cannot be delivered, compute does not happen.

8/ That is the layer NRG Bloom is building around: where stranded energy, modular infrastructure, and real compute demand finally meet.

9/ In the next wave of compute infrastructure, what matters more: customer demand or usable energy at the point of deployment?
