# LinkedIn Company Post
**Title:** The Constraint Is Not Demand. It Is Energy Delivery.
**Channel:** Company Page

Most people still talk about African compute as a demand story. That misses the real problem.

IEA says data-centre electricity demand could more than double by 2030. World Bank materials still describe Nigeria as having the world's largest electricity-access deficit.

The hard part is not whether demand exists. The hard part is whether energy can actually be delivered where compute can use it.

What that tells us:

- Compute is moving into a period where electricity availability matters more than slideware.
- Nigeria forces you to separate energy supply from energy delivery. They are not the same thing.
- NRG Bloom's model matters because it goes to trapped or wasted energy instead of waiting for the grid to behave.

The market does not need another polished Africa data-centre narrative. It needs people who understand that if power cannot be delivered, compute does not happen.

That is the layer NRG Bloom is building around: where stranded energy, modular infrastructure, and real compute demand finally meet.
