# LinkedIn Founder Post
**Title:** NGFCP Turned Flare Monetization Into a Governed Market
**Channel:** Founder POV
**Pillar:** flare-gas

Flare-gas compute in Nigeria should not be framed like a fringe experiment anymore.

By December 2025, NGFCP reporting pointed to 28 final permits, 49 awarded sites, and nearly 3 GW of power potential.

That is not just a talking point.
It points to the deeper truth: The category is moving from theory into a governed market. NGFCP made that visible, Green Flare proved other players are mobilizing, and Crusoe's North America arc proved the model itself was never imaginary.

Here is what operators see that commentators miss:

- NUPRC's permit process gives flare monetization a more legible commercial frame than most outsiders realize.
- Green Flare's 53 MW announcement shows the Nigerian market is already attracting public flare-to-compute build-outs.
- Crusoe's 2025 divestiture matters because it confirms the model was proven strongly enough to become part of a larger power-and-compute consolidation story.

The most interesting question is no longer whether flare-to-compute is real. It is who will build the African operators investors trust once the category becomes legible.

That is why we think the next generation of African compute will not be won by whoever has the biggest slide deck.
It will be won by whoever can finance, deploy, and operate remote infrastructure with discipline in the field.

NRG Bloom is positioning itself as a serious operator in a market that is becoming more regulated, more visible, and more competitive.

What gives investors more conviction in stranded-gas compute: regulation, basin economics, or proven field execution?
