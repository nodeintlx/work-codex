# X Thread

1/ Nigeria's data-centre story is real, but the more interesting point is how early the market still is.

2/ Market coverage in 2025 said Nigeria had about 65.8 MW of third-party data-centre capacity and about 327.8 MW more in the pipeline. That is the clue. The market is no longer empty, but it is still small enough that modular edge operators with real site access can create meaningful infrastructure before the landscape hardens.

3/ Most people think the constraint is demand. It isn't. The constraint is remote execution: The capacity that exists today is still small relative to the country's population, digital demand, and power constraints.

4/ Then there is capital discipline: Pipeline growth is real, but large centralized capacity does not eliminate the need for distributed energy-edge infrastructure.

5/ And finally speed: NRG Bloom's 1.5 MW operating proof matters because this category will not be won only by the biggest campus. It will also be won by operators who can monetize constrained or stranded energy where it already exists.

6/ Our contrarian take: The mistake is assuming the whole market resolves into hyperscale campuses. Nigeria is still early enough for smaller, disciplined operators to define valuable pieces of the stack.

7/ NRG Bloom is building a voice around the part of the digital-infrastructure market where energy realism and modular execution still matter.

8/ As Nigeria's data-centre market matures, where will the most defensible value sit: hyperscale campuses, modular edge deployments, or the link between the two?
