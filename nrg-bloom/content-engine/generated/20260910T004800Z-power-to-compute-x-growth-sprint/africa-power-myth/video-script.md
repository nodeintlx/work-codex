# Short Video Script
**Title:** Nigeria Does Not Have a Power Shortage. It Has a Delivery Problem.
**Format:** 45-60 second talking-head or kinetic text

## Hook
Nigeria is not short of power. It is short of wires.

## Body
14 GW of installed capacity in Nigeria. Roughly 4 GW actually delivered on an average day. The gap is not fuel. It is everything between the turbine and the socket.

Most people hear that and think market size.
What matters underneath the headline is simpler:

Site the compute at the source and the transmission failure that defines Nigerian power stops being your risk and starts being your discount.

First: The IEA put Nigeria's installed generation capacity at 14 GW in 2024 with average daily available capacity of 4.14 GW. That is a delivery failure, not a fuel failure.

Second: World Bank materials put roughly 85 to 90 million Nigerians without grid access, with the economic cost of poor supply estimated in the tens of billions of dollars a year.

Third: In the same country, gas is being flared at the wellhead. The energy is present and already at surface. The wires are the thing that is missing.

Almost every argument for why African compute is impossible turns out, when you push on it, to be an argument about transmission. Behind-the-meter compute does not use transmission.

## Close
Put the compute at the source and the wires stop mattering.
