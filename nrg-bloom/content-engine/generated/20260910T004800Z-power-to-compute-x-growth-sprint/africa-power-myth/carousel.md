# Carousel Brief — Nigeria Does Not Have a Power Shortage. It Has a Delivery Problem.

## Slide 1
- Heading: Nigeria Does Not Have a Power Shortage. It Has a Delivery Problem.
- Body: The lazy take is that Africa cannot power data centers. The real story is narrower and far more useful: Nigeria cannot move power, which is a completely different problem if you are willing to build at the source.

## Slide 2
- Heading: The Stat
- Body: 14 GW of installed capacity in Nigeria. Roughly 4 GW actually delivered on an average day. The gap is not fuel. It is everything between the turbine and the socket.

## Slide 3
- Heading: What Most People Miss
- Body: Site the compute at the source and the transmission failure that defines Nigerian power stops being your risk and starts being your discount.

## Slide 4
- Heading: Operator Truth #1
- Body: The IEA put Nigeria's installed generation capacity at 14 GW in 2024 with average daily available capacity of 4.14 GW. That is a delivery failure, not a fuel failure.

## Slide 5
- Heading: Operator Truth #2
- Body: World Bank materials put roughly 85 to 90 million Nigerians without grid access, with the economic cost of poor supply estimated in the tens of billions of dollars a year.

## Slide 6
- Heading: Operator Truth #3
- Body: In the same country, gas is being flared at the wellhead. The energy is present and already at surface. The wires are the thing that is missing.

## Slide 7
- Heading: The Contrarian Take
- Body: Almost every argument for why African compute is impossible turns out, when you push on it, to be an argument about transmission. Behind-the-meter compute does not use transmission.

## Slide 8
- Heading: Call To Action
- Body: That is the entire reason NRG Bloom deploys containers at the wellhead instead of queuing for a grid connection.
