# Data Sheet — The AI Buildout Became a Land Grab for Energized Sites. Africa Is Not in the Auction.

Brief slug: who-actually-has-the-power
Hero stat: IEA expects data centre electricity demand to more than double by 2030 to around 945 TWh. Africa's digital economy is projected near $712 billion by 2050. Those two lines do not currently meet anywhere.
Core thesis: Compute follows power, and power arbitrage is the most durable edge in this cycle. The arbitrage is widest exactly where the energy is trapped.

## Claims Used In The Post

- Hook claim: The AI buildout has quietly turned into a global land grab for anything with a power connection. The cheapest energized land on the planet is in places the auction has not reached yet.
- Proof point 1: The IEA projects global data centre electricity demand more than doubling by 2030 to roughly 945 TWh. That demand has to physically land somewhere with power.
- Proof point 2: Africa's digital economy is projected around $712 billion by 2050, yet Nigeria's third-party data centre base was reported near 65.8 MW live in 2025.
- Proof point 3: Capital is competing hard for scarce energized sites in mature markets while permitted, already-flowing gas sits unmonetized in the Niger Delta.
- Contrarian take: Frontier markets are not a discount version of this trade. They are the only place left where you can still buy the input cheap, and the reason it looks risky is that almost nobody has operated there.
