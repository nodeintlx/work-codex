That is not just a talking point.
It points to the deeper truth: North Dakota matters because it combined associated-gas scale, hard capture targets, and real infrastructure build-out under pressure.

Here is what operators see that commentators miss:

- North Dakota's policy framework kept pushing gas capture higher while production stayed large.
- That is exactly the kind of environment where flare-to-compute solutions either prove themselves or get exposed.
- NRG Bloom should speak about African deployment with the same seriousness that Bakken operators brought to gas handling and utilization.

The lesson from North Dakota is not just that flare mitigation is possible. It is that policy pressure and infrastructure constraint can create the conditions for category winners.

That is why we think the next generation of African compute will not be won by whoever has the biggest slide deck.
It will be won by whoever can finance, deploy, and operate remote infrastructure with discipline in the field.

NRG Bloom is studying the Bakken as an operating blueprint, not just as industry trivia.

What matters more in stranded-gas compute adoption: regulatory pressure, gas scale, or deployment speed?
