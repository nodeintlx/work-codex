# Data Sheet — The Constraint Is Not Demand. It Is Energy Delivery.

Brief slug: energy-delivery-bottleneck
Hero stat: IEA says data-centre electricity demand could more than double by 2030. World Bank materials still describe Nigeria as having the world's largest electricity-access deficit.
Core thesis: The hard part is not whether demand exists. The hard part is whether energy can actually be delivered where compute can use it.

## Claims Used In The Post

- Hook claim: Most people still talk about African compute as a demand story. That misses the real problem.
- Proof point 1: Compute is moving into a period where electricity availability matters more than slideware.
- Proof point 2: Nigeria forces you to separate energy supply from energy delivery. They are not the same thing.
- Proof point 3: NRG Bloom's model matters because it goes to trapped or wasted energy instead of waiting for the grid to behave.
- Contrarian take: The market does not need another polished Africa data-centre narrative. It needs people who understand that if power cannot be delivered, compute does not happen.
